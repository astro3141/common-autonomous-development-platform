## 1. Position and thinness rule

The withdrawn Conductor integration (PRs 199–204) failed by constitutionalizing an orchestrator's runtime; every Conductor concept demanded a CADP counterpart (Design D0). This document is its replacement at a fraction of that size, and the size bound is structural, not stylistic: per Spec v0.5 §4.1, mutation intents arriving as `EffectRequestV1` through an effect client is the **whole** mandatory integration between any workflow plane and CADP. Everything below specifies that boundary and nothing behind it. A Conductor workflow, a cron job, and a human at a terminal are equal citizens at this boundary (Spec v0.5 §4.2); nothing here is Conductor-specific except measured facts grounding refusals (§5, §6).

## 2. Scope fence (non-goals)

Per Design D7-2 and D7-3, this document contains, normatively:

- **no orchestrator task states** — how a Conductor (or Temporal, or cron) run represents progress is its business;
- **no retry semantics** — the orchestrator may retry its own steps freely; the *effect* retry discipline is the Authority Plane's (Spec v0.5 §4.4: no blind retry), stated there, not here;
- **no checkpoint model** — orchestrator durability is never authority (Spec v0.5 §4.1);
- **no DAG vocabulary** and **no Conductor YAML schema** — workflow shape never crosses the boundary.

Orchestrator internals appear below **only** as measured facts grounding refusals (Design preamble; §5, §6). If a future revision of this document states any of the above normatively, that is a regression to the withdrawn approach and grounds for REQUEST_CHANGES on its own.

## 3. The external-candidate effect client

### 3.1 Measured gap (P0)

v0.4 exposes no surface for an externally-produced candidate to enter governance. The live surfaces — `cadp/live/mcpServer.ts` (`cadp_work_start`) and `cadp/live/ctl.ts` — only start the internal Temporal pipeline: `cadpWork` (`cadp/product/workflows.ts`) runs its own `implementCandidate` / `verifyCandidate` / `reviewCandidate` activities. A candidate produced anywhere else (a Conductor workflow's working tree, a cron job, a human clone) has no path to the governed effects `GIT_PUSH`, `PR_CREATE`, `PR_MERGE` except by not being governed at all. This section closes that gap with the minimal caller surface.

### 3.2 Caller surface — no new API, no caller-contract delta

The client is the existing Kernel API of TD v0.4 §12, unchanged: `put_blob`, `allocate_effect_id`, `seal_effect_request`, `submit_evidence`, `assemble_admission_input`, `evaluate`, `admit_and_dispatch`, `get_effect_state`, `request_reconcile`, plus the reads. An external effect proposer authenticates as its own registered principal (mTLS/SPIFFE or, for a human terminal, the IdP path) with the **workflow/orchestrator caller class reach** of the TD v0.4 §12 matrix. No new endpoint, no new record kind — and, correcting the prior draft, **no caller-contract (method-reach) delta either**. The implemented method-reach contract (`METHOD_REACH`, `cadp/kernel/api.ts`) is a **static method-to-`process_class` table**: it is code, not registry content — `cadp.kernel-config.v1`'s closed schema carries no method-reach key — and it is **kind-blind**: `submit_evidence` reach already exists for the `worker`, `evidence-adapter`, `human-surface`, and `agent-surface` process classes (alongside `workflow` and `deployment-control`). The per-evidence-kind restriction lives one layer down, enforced by the Ingress on every submission: authenticated principal → `identity_registry` entry (exact string; `process_class`; `producer_ref`) → the `adapter_registry` entry for that exact `producer_ref` → `evidence_kinds.includes(kind)` and the registered `source_relation` (TD v0.4 §9.1; `cadp/kernel/ingress.ts`).

The submission contract is therefore explicit: **the actual candidate producer submits `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` directly, as its own registered principal, authenticating as a process class that ALREADY holds `submit_evidence` method reach** — never relayed through the orchestrator, whose relay would seal as `SELF_REPORT` of the orchestrator (§4.1). A deployment that gates on candidate-producer provenance declares exactly **two** registry deltas, and nothing else:

- **(a) `identity_registry`:** exact registration of the producing principal — exact SPIFFE ID or IdP subject, string equality, no patterns — with its `identity_class` (whose `process_class` is one that already holds `submit_evidence` reach) and its exact `producer_ref` (TD v0.4 §5.4; §9.1 S2). An unregistered producer cannot attest; there is no anonymous or pattern-matched attestation path.
- **(b) `adapter_registry`:** the entry for that exact `producer_ref` gains `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` in its `evidence_kinds` (TD v0.4 §9.1), no wildcard.

**One `source_relation` per `producer_ref` — dedicated provenance producers.** The implemented registry entry shape is `{ producer_ref, evidence_kinds[], source_relation, produced_at_source }` (`REFERENCE_ADAPTERS`, `cadp/deployment/referencePolicy.ts`): **one `source_relation` per `producer_ref`, not one per evidence kind**, and the Ingress requires the submitted draft's relation to EQUAL that single registered value (`cadp/kernel/ingress.ts`: `adapter.source_relation !== draft.source_relation` → `SOURCE_RELATION_FORBIDDEN`). Therefore the `producer_ref` that submits self-reported `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` **MUST be an `adapter_registry` entry whose `source_relation` is `SELF_REPORT`**, and a principal whose existing producer entry carries any other `source_relation` **registers a SEPARATE, DEDICATED `producer_ref` for provenance submission** — both deltas (a) and (b) then apply to that dedicated entry, not to the existing one. Concretely: `reviewer:codex` is registered `INDEPENDENT_OBSERVATION` for `REVIEW` (`REFERENCE_ADAPTERS`), so adding a `SELF_REPORT` `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` to that same entry is inexpressible — the submission is refused at ingress, or the entry's relation is flipped and every `REVIEW` that producer submits is corrupted into `SELF_REPORT`. The identical rule governs the stronger provenance sources of §4.2: an independent-observation provenance producer is likewise its own dedicated entry, registered `INDEPENDENT_OBSERVATION`. Making `source_relation` per-evidence-kind would be an Authority Plane registry-schema redesign, out of scope here.

Both are **ordinary policy-bundle changes, gate-controlled like any other registry change** (TD v0.4 §9.4): they ride a `POLICY_ACTIVATE`, never a runtime toggle. **Boundary note:** only a deployment that needs to open `submit_evidence` to a process class that does NOT already hold it would touch the static caller contract — and that is a code-level Authority Plane change, outside `POLICY_ACTIVATE`, out of scope here. This surface remains safe for untrusted callers for exactly the reason TD v0.4 §12 states: no method grants authority — a sealed request still needs evidence, an evaluator decision under the active policy, and the PEP's fresh recheck (Spec v0.5 §4.3 steps 5–7). `requester_ref` is always stamped from the authenticated principal, never accepted from the body (TD v0.4 §9.1 S3).

### 3.3 Effect identity — Platform-sealed, orchestrator-retry-convergent

`effect_id` is allocated by Platform effect ingress before any non-trivial external call; a caller-proposed correlation string is never identity authority (Spec v0.5 K3). Concretely for external proposers:

- The proposer calls `allocate_effect_id` with a versioned canonical tuple (TD v0.4 §7.4 discipline: canonical JSON, closed `purpose` vocabulary, no raw concatenation), schema `cadp.allocation-key.external.v1`: `{ schema, repo_id, candidate_base_sha, candidate_sha, purpose }`. `candidate_base_sha` (§3.4 vocabulary: the exact base revision the candidate was built and reviewed against) is an exact immutable identity component alongside repository identity and `candidate_sha` — without it, two submissions whose candidates sit on materially different base revisions could share an `effect_id`, contradicting K3's binding of one `effect_id` to one `request_digest` over exact material. The Ingress scopes the derived key by the **stamped** requester principal — two principals proposing the same candidate get distinct effect identities; a retry by the same principal converges on the same `effect_id`, so an orchestrator that re-runs a step never creates a second logical effect. (This replaces the internal tuple's `work_run_ref + step_ordinal` scoping, which presumes run-profile enrollment — §5 refuses that here.)
- **Allocation-to-first-seal binding (closes the pre-K3 window).** K3's one-`effect_id`-one-`request_digest` conflict rule presupposes an *existing* sealed `request_digest`; on the first `seal_effect_request` for an allocated `effect_id` there is nothing yet to conflict with, so K3 alone would not stop a first seal naming different material than the allocation — or a first seal by a different principal than the allocator. This document therefore states the **observable cross-plane contract, and only that**: (a) an allocation MUST be scoped to the stamped requester principal and MUST be durable across restart; (b) the FIRST `seal_effect_request` for an `effect_id` MUST verify the allocation binding — stamped principal, `repo_id`, `candidate_base_sha`, `candidate_sha`, and the closed purpose-to-`operation_kind` correspondence (purpose `git-push` ↔ `operation_kind` `GIT_PUSH`, `pr-create` ↔ `PR_CREATE`, `pr-merge` ↔ `PR_MERGE`, the purpose literals coming from the policy bundle's closed `allocation_purposes` vocabulary — `cadp.kernel-config.v1`, TD v0.4 §5.4 — never invented by the caller) — **atomically with respect to the allocation**, so that a mismatch is REFUSED deterministically BEFORE any K3 record exists, including under concurrent first-seal attempts, never degenerating into a post-hoc K3 conflict whose semantics vary by implementation. On refusal, no `EffectRequestV1` is sealed and no effect is performed. The binding is explicitly **not** a new constitutional record — no K8 is created. **The storage schema, transaction, and locking that realize this contract are owned by the Authority Plane TD**, to which this document delegates them (per its own Non-scope; §2). Measured seam, marked as an Authority Plane TD work item: today `allocate_effect_id` does not pass the authenticated principal to the Ingress — `cadp/kernel/api.ts` passes the principal only for `seal_effect_request` and `submit_evidence` — so requester-scoped allocation is not yet realized at that seam. Once the first sealed `request_digest` exists, the ordinary K3 conflict rule remains the guard for everything after it — same material converges, different material conflicts and performs no effect (Spec v0.5 K3). The binding closes the pre-K3 window; K3 governs everything after it.
- A Conductor run identifier is doubly disqualified as an identity component: constitutionally, because K3 forbids caller identity authority; and factually, because P0 measured that `CONDUCTOR_RUN_ID` is not exported to script-step environments despite the documentation saying it is. It may travel as opaque diagnostic content inside `source_ref`/claim payloads; it never keys anything.

### 3.4 The three governed effects and their material bindings

All three re-use the v0.4 GitHub adapter contract of TD v0.4 §6.4 **byte-for-byte** — same materials, same idempotency/precondition rules, same COMMITTED / NO_EFFECT_CONFIRMED proofs. What is new is only who seals the request.

**Vocabulary split — two different "bases".** `candidate_base_sha` is the immutable provenance/input binding: it records which base revision the candidate was built and reviewed against, and it is what the §3.3 allocation tuple and the sealed `work_bindings` carry. `base_ref` is the mutable PR/merge target branch at the target. No existing adapter proves that `base_ref`'s current revision equals `candidate_base_sha` at effect time: `PR_CREATE`'s `base_ref` is a mutable branch that can move between seal and call, and the GitHub merge API's `sha` precondition is a CAS on the PR HEAD, never on the base branch.

| Effect | Material (TD v0.4 §6.4) | External-candidate binding rule |
|---|---|---|
| `GIT_PUSH` | `{repo_id, ref = refs/heads/cadp/candidate/<candidate_sha>, new_sha = candidate_sha, expected_old_sha = 0000…, bundle_cas_key}` | The proposer `put_blob`s a `git bundle` of the candidate; the PEP verifies the bundle reproduces **exactly** `candidate_sha` before pushing (TD v0.4 §6.6, §8.1). An external bundle is exactly as untrusted as a worker bundle was; the same verification makes it safe. The sealed request also binds `candidate_base_sha` as exact immutable input (rule below); the PEP's ancestry check runs against exactly that admitted `candidate_base_sha` (TD v0.4 §6.4) — bundle ancestry against the admitted base is target-state-independent and enforceable, so this effect's base discipline is complete as stated. |
| `PR_CREATE` | `{repo_id, base_ref, head_ref = refs/heads/cadp/candidate/<head_sha>, head_sha, title_cas_key, body_cas_key}` | `head_sha = candidate_sha`; write-once candidate ref shape enforced at admission (TD v0.4 §4.4 #14). The sealed request also binds `candidate_base_sha` as provenance (rule below). `base_ref` is the mutable target branch; this effect does not prove what revision it names at call time. |
| `PR_MERGE` | `{repo_id, pr_number, expected_head_sha, merge_method}` | `expected_head_sha = candidate_sha`; the target-native CAS rejects **head** drift only — GitHub's `sha` precondition is on the PR HEAD, never on the base branch. The sealed request also binds `candidate_base_sha` as provenance (rule below). |

For each of the three effects, `candidate_base_sha` and repository identity enter the sealed `EffectRequestV1` alongside `candidate_sha` as exact immutable bindings: `work_bindings` carries them as exact input identity + revision, covered by `request_digest` (Spec v0.5 K3), and equal by the §3.3 allocation-to-first-seal binding to the allocated values. This is the same exact-input discipline TD v0.4 §8.3 applies to the candidate subject in the development composition — equality over K4-bound sealed facts, decided by the evaluator; inequality = requirement unsatisfied, no K6 — extended to the base revision that composition already pins (worker workspace materialized at exactly `candidate_base_sha`, bundle ancestry checked against the admitted base; TD v0.4 §8.1, §6.4). What this binding proves is **provenance, not target state**: policy and reviewers evaluate against the pinned base the candidate was built and reviewed on; the binding does not — and no measured adapter can — pin the target branch's current revision at effect time. A policy that cares about the target's *current* base takes a fresh target observation (`base_ref` → current sha) into K4 as evidence and applies its drift rule there, evaluator-side (Spec v0.5 §2.4). Atomic exact-base-at-merge enforcement is a target adapter contract gap: `[UNMEASURED — probe required: no GitHub-native atomic base-branch CAS exists in the measured v0.4 adapter contract]`. Any ancestry rule (candidate descends from `candidate_base_sha`) is development-composition semantics per Spec v0.5 §7, evaluated by the evaluator over K4-bound facts — never new kernel vocabulary.

### 3.5 Re-homed unchanged

- **Broker push/PR actuation:** the credential-less bounded broker and PEP-held GitHub credential (TD v0.4 §4, §18.2) actuate; the proposer never touches a governed-target credential (§6).
- **Gate-file routing:** `touchesGateMachinery` (`cadp/product/gateFiles.ts`) applies to external candidates identically — a candidate touching kernel/policy/surface/conformance/constitutional-document paths routes to a `HUMAN_DECISION`, never a delegated agent merge.
- **Agent vs HUMAN merge decision:** the TD v0.4 §12 r9 `agent-surface` contract carries over — `AGENT_DECISION` satisfies only the merge gate, only for producers named in `delegated_merge_producers`, formed by a fresh isolated reviewer, honestly attributed. For an external candidate the carried-over r9 predicate is, and claims only, **proposer/reviewer separation**: a delegate that is, or shares an identity class with, the proposing `requester_ref` is refused. Proposer/reviewer separation is not implementer independence. Where the active policy additionally requires **implementer/reviewer independence**, that predicate evaluates against the separation basis of §4.2 — the attested `producer_refs[]` set of the candidate's provenance evidence (extended by observed authorship only in a deployment that declares the §4.2 git-identity alias binding contract) — and it is exactly the **implemented reference predicate** (`independent_product` and its call sites in `review_ok` / `agent_merge_ok`, `cadp/deployment/referencePolicy.ts`):

> the reviewer's/delegate's `producer_ref` MUST NOT be a member of `producer_refs[]`, **AND** for every `p` in `producer_refs[]`, `identity_registry[p].identity_class.product != identity_registry[reviewer_producer_ref].identity_class.product`.

That is **`identity_class.product` inequality, not whole-`identity_class` inequality**, and the difference is load-bearing: the same `product` under a different `account` or `process_class` PASSES whole-object inequality and is DENIED by the implemented product rule — the exact self-merge shape the live self-approval fix closed (the 12th/13th pilot findings recorded in `referencePolicy.ts`, where a claude-implemented run was auto-merged by the claude-product delegate). Proposer/reviewer separation is retained on top, never replaced. When that evidence is absent, or its `producer_refs[]` set is empty, the independence requirement is unsatisfied and no K6 is created (Spec v0.5 §2.5) — an empty producer set proves no independence (`count(implementer_refs) > 0`, `referencePolicy.ts`; TD v0.4 §8.3). And per the §4.2 assurance decision, for an **external candidate** a delegated `AGENT_DECISION` merge is available only when that independence is established from a provenance source stronger than self-report; absent such a source the merge decision falls back to `HUMAN_DECISION`.

## 4. Evidence submission

### 4.1 Ingress and provenance honesty

External evidence enters as `EvidenceEnvelopeV1` through `submit_evidence`, under the full TD v0.4 §9.1 ingress discipline: `producer_ref` stamped from the authenticated principal; `provenance.integrity` set by the Ingress; `source_relation` checked against the policy-bound adapter registry. The consequence for orchestrator-relayed evidence is mechanical: **when an orchestrator submits a review it ran (the P0 case: a codex review executed inside the Conductor loop), the envelope's producer is the orchestrator's principal and its relation is at most `SELF_REPORT` of the orchestrator** — unless the platform can independently verify the actual producer (a signature the Ingress verifies against `data.cadp.attestation_keys` → `SIGNED_ATTESTATION` under the true producer, or the producer submitting directly as its own registered principal). Self-report is never promoted (Spec v0.5 §4.5); an honest `SELF_REPORT` review is recorded, and simply does not satisfy a gate requiring independence.

### 4.2 What the reference policy accepts for external candidates

The candidate-sha subject-equality rules of TD v0.4 §8.3 (C41 leg 1) apply unchanged — they are composition semantics now (Spec v0.5 §2.4, §7): a required `REVIEW`/`VERIFICATION` envelope satisfies the requirement only if its bound `repo_id + candidate_sha` equals the exact candidate the sealed material names; inequality is requirement-unsatisfied, no K6.

**Candidate-producer provenance (`CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE`).** Proving reviewer ≠ proposer does not prove reviewer ≠ actual implementer: in the Conductor case the proposer is principal C while the actual coder is some agent X, and a reviewer R with R ≠ C proves nothing about R vs X. Spec v0.5's independence generalization requires separation from the producer(s) of the exact subject/material being approved, not from the requester. External candidates therefore carry actual candidate-producer provenance as an **ordinary evidence kind** — no new kernel primitive; a gate consumes it, so it is not orchestrator telemetry: a `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` `EvidenceEnvelopeV1` whose subject binds `{ repo_id, candidate_sha }` exactly and whose claim binds `producer_refs[]` — the **complete** set of exact `producer_ref` values that produced any of the candidate's content, never "a" producer. The claim speaks the **`producer_ref` identity domain**, deliberately: K2 seals `producer_ref` — the Ingress resolves the authenticated principal through `identity_registry` and stamps `identity.producer_ref` into the envelope; Spec v0.5 K2 carries no principal field — and the §8.4 predicates and reviewer envelopes all speak `producer_ref`, so a set of principals would force an un-contracted `producer_ref`-to-principal reverse mapping whose 1:1 uniqueness no kernel config guarantees. An orchestrator-relayed claim of who produced the candidate is `SELF_REPORT` **of the orchestrator** and does not establish the producer set — the same rule §4.1 applies to reviews. The authorized submitter exists only by declaration: the actual candidate producer submits this kind **directly, as its own registered principal**, under the two §3.2 registry deltas — exact `identity_registry` registration of the producing principal, and the `adapter_registry` entry for its exact `producer_ref` carrying the kind in `evidence_kinds`. A policy requiring `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` while no registered `producer_ref` carries the kind in `adapter_registry.evidence_kinds` is the unsatisfiable configuration — no authorized caller can produce the required evidence — and the reference policy's authoring tests reject it (general rule below).

**Assurance decision — self-report is audit evidence, never an independence gate's basis.** A producer attesting who produced the candidate is self-reporting by construction, and the complete-set contract below admits honestly that the platform cannot *detect* an omitted co-producer from the attestation alone. That residual is not academic: a candidate produced by P1+P2 attested as `[P1]` lets P2 review and merge it, and P2 can be the very principal that submitted the attestation omitting its own ref. Attributability is post-hoc accountability, not prevention — and Spec v0.5 makes separation from the **actual** producer(s) of the exact subject/material a MUST for delegated machine decisions, with self-report never promoted (§4.3, §4.5). The reference policy therefore fixes four rules:

- **(a) Accepted, but as attribution.** `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` submitted directly by its registered producer as `source_relation` `SELF_REPORT` with `provenance.integrity` `AUTHENTICATED_SOURCE` is accepted as **ATTRIBUTABLE PROVENANCE / AUDIT evidence** — recorded in K2, subject-bound to the exact `{ repo_id, candidate_sha }`, and accountable to its stamped `producer_ref`. It is **NOT sufficient assurance for an independence gate**, whose whole purpose is preventing machine self-approval. Self-report is never promoted (Spec v0.5 §4.5).
- **(b) Delegated machine merge needs a stronger source.** For an external candidate, delegated `AGENT_DECISION` merge is available only when the policy-required implementer/reviewer independence (the §3.5 predicate) is established from a **stronger provenance source**, of which exactly three are contemplated: **(i)** independently produced provenance — an envelope from a producer whose *registered* `source_relation` is `INDEPENDENT_OBSERVATION` (its own dedicated `adapter_registry` entry, §3.2) observing who produced the candidate; **(ii)** a verified `SIGNED_ATTESTATION` — `provenance.integrity` set by the Ingress from a signature verified against `data.cadp.attestation_keys`, not asserted in the draft (`cadp/kernel/ingress.ts`: `provenance` is a `DRAFT_FIELD_FORBIDDEN` top-level key); **(iii)** Execution Plane evidence binding the candidate output to the executing producer identity — the Spec v0.5 §6 `output_artifact_digest` binding compared against the sealed material's artifact rather than inferred from K4 inclusion (Spec v0.5 §2.4 execution-provenance strengthening). Availability of (iii): `[UNMEASURED — probe required: the Execution Plane capture probe]`.
- **(c) Fail closed to the Human otherwise.** Absent such a source, the merge decision for an external candidate is `HUMAN_DECISION` (TD v0.4 §9.3 path A). **This is the reference-policy default for external candidates today** — not a degraded mode and not a deployment's oversight: no reference producer is registered to supply (i) or (ii) for candidate provenance, and (iii) is unmeasured, so the reference deployment's external-candidate merge path is the Human path until one of them is registered and measured.
- **(d) Necessary-but-insufficient ingress rule: no self-omitting attester.** For every `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` submission whose producer's *registered* `source_relation` is `SELF_REPORT` (§3.2), the envelope's **stamped `producer_ref` MUST be a member of its own `claim.producer_refs[]`**; an attester excluding itself from the set it attests is REFUSED at ingress, as a kind-specific ingress rule alongside the existing ones (`cadp/kernel/ingress.ts`, where `producer_ref` is stamped from the authenticated identity and never accepted from the body). This is explicitly marked **necessary, not sufficient**: it kills only the self-omission shape, and does **not** close third-party co-producer omission — a P1 attestation naming `[P1]` while P2 also produced content is refused by nothing at ingress. It is why (a)–(c), not (d), carry the assurance. Its scope is fixed by the **registered `source_relation` alone and is blind to `provenance.integrity`**: `source_relation` and `integrity` are orthogonal axes (`cadp/kernel/records.ts`: `"SELF_REPORT" | "INDEPENDENT_OBSERVATION" | "TARGET_AUTHORITY_OBSERVATION"` against `"UNATTESTED" | "AUTHENTICATED_SOURCE" | "SIGNED_ATTESTATION"`), so a self-report that the Ingress raises to a verified `SIGNED_ATTESTATION` under (b)(ii) is **equally bound** — signing an attestation never buys the right to omit yourself from the set you attest, and (b)(ii) strengthens *how* a claim is attributed, never *what* it must contain. The rule binds the self-attestation relation and nothing else: a producer registered `INDEPENDENT_OBSERVATION` (or `TARGET_AUTHORITY_OBSERVATION`) under (b)(i) is not a producer of the candidate, so its assurance comes from its registered relation and its own **non**-membership in the set it reports, and requiring self-membership of it would be incoherent.

**Complete-set contract.** A single-producer field would reopen, at the external boundary, the self-approval hole TD v0.4 §8.3 closed internally by collecting **all** `BACKEND_EXECUTION` producers into `implementer_refs`: a candidate co-produced by P1 and P2, attested naming only P1, would let P2 review it and pass a reviewer-≠-named-producer check. The claim therefore binds the complete producer set, and the attesting principal **attests completeness**: an attestation that omits a producer is a false attestation, attributable to that principal exactly as any authenticated/signed K2 evidence is attributable to its stamped `producer_ref` (Spec v0.5 K2; TD v0.4 §9.1). Stated honestly: the platform cannot *detect* an omitted co-producer from the attestation alone — the K2 integrity model makes omission attributable, not impossible. An **empty** `producer_refs[]` fails closed: it satisfies no independence requirement, because a vacuous universal quantifier is not a proof of independence (the TD v0.4 §8.3 discipline: `count(implementer_refs) > 0`). Identity classes of `producer_refs[]` members are derived through the active policy's identity registry from each `producer_ref` (TD v0.4 §9.1 S2), never read from the claim.

**Separation basis.** Every implementer/reviewer independence predicate in §3.5 and this section evaluates against the **full** attested `producer_refs[]` set, in the `producer_ref` domain end to end, and is exactly the implemented reference predicate (`independent_product` and its call sites in `review_ok` / `agent_merge_ok`, `cadp/deployment/referencePolicy.ts`):

> the reviewer's/delegate's `producer_ref` MUST NOT be a member of `producer_refs[]`, **AND** for every `p` in `producer_refs[]`, `identity_registry[p].identity_class.product != identity_registry[reviewer_producer_ref].identity_class.product`.

**`identity_class.product` inequality, not whole-`identity_class` inequality** — same product, different `account` or `process_class` passes the whole-object test and is DENIED by the implemented product rule, which is the self-merge shape the live self-approval fix closed. Both `identity_class` values are derived through the active registry from the `producer_ref` (TD v0.4 §9.1 S2), never read from any claim. Proposer/reviewer separation (§3.5) is retained on top, never replaced. When the evidence is absent, or `producer_refs[]` is empty, and the policy requires implementer/reviewer independence, the requirement is unsatisfied and no K6 is created (Spec v0.5 §2.5) — and per the assurance decision above, satisfying this predicate from `SELF_REPORT` provenance alone does not make a delegated machine merge available for an external candidate.

**Consumption rule for multiple or conflicting provenance envelopes (observable cross-plane contract).** K2 is append-only, so one candidate can accumulate several `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` envelopes carrying different `producer_refs[]` sets from different producers; and K4's `evidence_refs[]` is caller-influenced, so a caller could present a favorable envelope and omit an unfavorable one. Cherry-picking the separation basis would defeat every predicate above. The rule is therefore:

- **(a) Authoritative producer designation.** The reference policy designates the authoritative provenance `producer_ref`(s) **per deployment**, as a policy-bundle fact riding a `POLICY_ACTIVATE` like any other registry/policy content (TD v0.4 §9.4) — never a runtime toggle and never caller-chosen.
- **(b) Assembly completeness is the Platform's obligation.** Admission-input assembly for an external-candidate effect MUST include **EVERY** sealed `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` envelope bound to the exact `{ repo_id, candidate_sha }` subject — not a caller-selected subset. Completeness is owed by the Platform at assembly, not by the caller's `evidence_refs[]`. **The storage schema and query mechanics that realize subject-complete assembly are owned by the Authority Plane TD**, to which this document delegates them — the same delegation pattern as §3.3's allocation-to-first-seal binding, and for the same reason: this document states the observable contract and not its implementation.
- **(c) The evaluator FAILS CLOSED on conflict.** If the assembled input carries more than one `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` for the subject and their claim digests are **not identical**, the independence requirement is **unsatisfied** — no K6. No picking the authoritative one, no union of the sets, no latest-wins: sealed history has no "latest" authority and "latest" lookups at dispatch are forbidden (Spec v0.5 §2.6). Identical-digest duplicates converge harmlessly and are not a conflict. Conflict is a signal that the deployment's provenance producers disagree about who made the candidate, which is precisely the condition under which no machine may claim independence from them.

**Observed authorship (PRODUCT DIAGNOSTICS ONLY absent a declared alias contract — not a K2 evidence item, not a gate input).** The PEP already walks `candidate_base_sha..candidate_sha` when it verifies that the `GIT_PUSH` bundle reproduces exactly `candidate_sha` (§3.4; TD v0.4 §6.6, §8.1), and the author/committer identity strings it observes in that walk are useful to operators. But until a deployment declares a git-identity alias binding contract, those observed strings are **product diagnostics only** — surfaced in logs and operator tooling, never sealed as K2 evidence and never consumed by any gate. A real K2 evidence kind for observed authorship would need its own claim schema, subject binding, producer registration, and seal path — none of which this document owns. The gap is structural: the TD v0.4 `identity_registry` is an exact-string match over SPIFFE IDs / IdP subjects with `identity_class` (§9.1 S2, §5.4); git Author/Committer strings (name + email) are neither, and no git-identity-to-registered-principal alias binding contract exists, so a mapping "through the policy identity registry" is not deterministically evaluable, and the evaluator's separation basis is the attested `producer_refs[]` set **alone**. `[UNMEASURED — probe required: no git-identity-to-principal alias binding exists in the v0.4 registry contract]`. A deployment that declares the alias contract (alias → registered principal/`producer_ref`, with that binding's own authority and provenance defined) thereby **defines that evidence kind and its registry entries in its own policy/Authority documents** — and only then do the following apply: the use stays one-directional, and stated honestly — commit authorship is producer-asserted content, anyone can commit under any name, so an observed identity string is **never identity proof**; the observed identities, mapped through the declared alias binding, only *extend* the separation basis, so an unreported co-producer visible in the authorship trail still blocks their own review even though the attestation omitted them. A co-producer whose authorship trail is forged or absent is not caught by this extension — that residual is covered only by the complete-set attestation accountability above, and the document marks it as residual rather than closed. Ordering (alias-contract deployments only): the observation comes into existence at `GIT_PUSH` bundle verification, so per this section's prerequisite matrix it can gate `PR_CREATE` and `PR_MERGE` but never `GIT_PUSH` itself, whose pre-push independence rests on the attested set alone.

**Per-effect evidence prerequisite matrix (normative).** Evidence can gate an effect only if it can exist before that effect: a GitHub Actions verification exists only after the candidate ref is pushed, so it cannot gate `GIT_PUSH`; a GitHub-native PR review exists only after the PR exists, so it cannot gate `PR_CREATE`. The reference policy gates per effect:

| Effect | Evidence the reference policy requires |
|---|---|
| `GIT_PUSH` | Only evidence obtainable pre-push: `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` (above), and any policy-required pre-push `REVIEW` by a registered principal over the submitted bundle/candidate content. |
| `PR_CREATE` | May additionally require: the `GIT_PUSH` outcome `COMMITTED`, and a `PRESENT` `VERIFICATION` from `verifier:github-actions` bound to the exact pushed `candidate_sha` with `claim.conclusion == "success"`, `TARGET_AUTHORITY_OBSERVATION`, source-authoritative `produced_at` (TD v0.4 §18.3 fail-closed projection, unchanged; this producer observes the pushed ref at the target, so it is independent of whoever made the candidate by construction). |
| `PR_MERGE` | May additionally require: PR-created facts; a GitHub-native review read via API (subject = `commit_id`, compared by the evaluator) or a platform review of the pushed ref, satisfying the implementer/reviewer independence predicate above (an orchestrator-relayed review only under the §4.1 verification paths — as bare relay it is `SELF_REPORT` and does not satisfy); and `HUMAN_DECISION` (TD v0.4 §9.3 path A) or delegated `AGENT_DECISION` per §3.5. |

General rule — a **REFERENCE-POLICY AUTHORING INVARIANT, not a kernel or activation contract**: no machine-readable effect-to-required-evidence or evidence-to-prerequisite dependency graph exists in `cadp.kernel-config.v1`, and policy is Rego, so no current contract can statically decide satisfiability of an arbitrary policy at `POLICY_ACTIVATE` time — the kernel and `POLICY_ACTIVATE` make NO general satisfiability judgment. The invariant is enforced by the reference policy's own unit/conformance tests: no required evidence for effect E has E itself (or a later effect) as its producing precondition, and every required evidence kind has at least one registered `producer_ref` carrying it in `adapter_registry.evidence_kinds` (§3.2). Honest safety note: an unsatisfiable policy is still fail-closed — it admits nothing — so the invariant protects liveness, not safety. If activation-time refusal is ever wanted, it requires machine-readable prerequisite metadata defined in the Authority Plane TD — out of scope here.

### 4.3 Fail-closed default

Absent, stale, subject-mismatched, or `UNKNOWN` required evidence admits nothing (Spec v0.5 §2.5, §4.5). There is no "external candidates get a lighter gate" mode: an external candidate passes the same evaluator, the same fresh recheck, and the same gate-file routing as an internal one, or it does not pass.

## 5. Explicit non-enrollment in the run profile

### 5.1 Measured facts (P0, run `24919dde`, 2026-09-07)

Conductor's run receipts are local-file grade: an `events.jsonl` under `TMPDIR`, keyed by an 8-hex `run_id`; the run record is **deleted on normal completion**; a checkpoint is written only on failure by default.

### 5.2 The refusal

Run-scope validity grades by the K7 outcome of a `WORK_START` effect (Spec v0.5 §5.2): `COMMITTED` requires a target-authoritative receipt binding the exact effect (the measured v0.4 rule: a describe-read returning a memo matching `cadp_effect_id` and `cadp_args_digest` — TD v0.4 §6.4, C34). A TMPDIR file deleted on success, keyed by a local 8-hex identifier, cannot be that receipt: there is no target-authoritative `WORK_START` outcome to grade a Conductor run scope by K7, and after a normal completion there is no receipt at all. **Therefore the reference deployment enrolls no Conductor principal in the run profile. Conductor runs claim per-effect Kernel Conformance only — never the run profile.** This is a measured refusal, not a policy preference, and it is complete governance, not degraded governance: per-effect Kernel Conformance is the whole constitutional guarantee (Spec v0.5 §5).

Non-enrollment is symmetric and policy-bound (Spec v0.5 §5.1): the active policy simply does not name any Conductor principal among the enrolled `requester_ref`s, and the reference policy reserves run-scope binding for enrolled principals — a non-enrolled principal **cannot acquire run-profile semantics by presenting a `work_run_ref`**. What non-enrollment costs is stated, not hidden: no `MAX_EFFECTS_IN_WORK_RUN`, no `WORK_STEP` chain requirements, no bound-stop semantics for these runs.

### 5.3 Revisit conditions

Enrollment could be revisited only on new measurement: `[UNMEASURED — probe required: a target-authoritative run receipt from the orchestrator — durable across normal completion, correlatable to a Platform-sealed WORK_START effect_id by a target-returned binding (memo-equivalent), and readable by the Reconciler after restart. Absent all three properties, K7 grading of a Conductor run scope remains impossible and non-enrollment stands.]`

## 6. Credential custody at the client (measured)

P0 measured that a Conductor `working_dir` is **not a sandbox**, and that `claude-agent-sdk` loads the working directory's settings as a trust boundary. The consequence is the Spec v0.5 §2.3 rule with no softening: **no governed-target credential may exist anywhere an orchestrator's working directory or settings can reach.** Credential custody stays entirely PEP/broker-side (TD v0.4 §4.1, §18.2: the GitHub credential is PEP-held; the broker is credential-less toward the kernel; workers cannot `admit_and_dispatch` — matrix reach notwithstanding, the worker class cannot even seal requests). The only CADP/governed-effect credential an effect-proposer environment holds is its own Kernel API principal identity, which grants no effect authority by §3.2. A deployment that places a governed-target token in a Conductor working directory has left Kernel Conformance for every effect that token can reach, whatever this document says.

## 7. Conformance controls (falsification additions)

In the Spec v0.5 §9 spirit — proven by attempting the violation:

1. **Replay convergence:** the same external principal re-calling `allocate_effect_id` with the same `cadp.allocation-key.external.v1` tuple receives the same `effect_id`; the target shows one push, one PR (counted at the target, TD v0.4 §13 method).
2. **Identity-squatting refusal:** a different principal submitting the same tuple receives a different `effect_id` and cannot conflict the first principal's request.
3. **Relayed-review honesty:** an orchestrator-relayed review seals as `SELF_REPORT` of the orchestrator principal and a gate requiring independent review DENYs; the same review submitted by the reviewer's own registered principal (or attested per §4.1) admits.
4. **Subject inequality:** review/verification bound to candidate A does not admit an effect naming candidate B (C41 leg 1 rerun against the external client).
5. **Opt-in refusal:** a non-enrolled principal's request carrying a fabricated `work_run_ref` is refused (Spec v0.5 §9.2 negative control, external edition).
6. **Gate-file guard:** an external candidate touching a `GATE_PATH_RULES` path cannot be merged by `AGENT_DECISION`.
7. **First-seal candidate binding:** an `effect_id` allocated for candidate A whose first `seal_effect_request` names candidate B is REFUSED — no `EffectRequestV1` sealed, no effect (§3.3 allocation-to-first-seal binding).
8. **First-seal base binding:** an `effect_id` allocated with `candidate_base_sha` X whose first seal names base Y is REFUSED.
9. **First-seal purpose binding:** an `effect_id` allocated with purpose `pr-create` whose first seal names `operation_kind` `PR_MERGE` is REFUSED (§3.3 closed `allocation_purposes` correspondence).
10. **First-seal principal binding:** principal B first-sealing an `effect_id` allocated to principal A is REFUSED.
11. **Unsatisfiable-authoring refusal (reference-policy tests, not activation):** the reference policy bundle's own authoring/conformance tests reject a requirement that is cyclic — e.g. requiring, for `GIT_PUSH`, the `verifier:github-actions` `VERIFICATION` of the pushed candidate, evidence whose producing precondition is the `GIT_PUSH` itself — and a requirement whose evidence kind no registered `producer_ref` carries in `adapter_registry.evidence_kinds` (§4.2 general rule). The control tests those authoring tests; it does NOT claim an activation-time refusal by the deployment — `POLICY_ACTIVATE` makes no satisfiability judgment.
12. **Producer-set self-review refusal (product predicate):** two legs, both DENYed (§4.2 separation basis; `independent_product`, `cadp/deployment/referencePolicy.ts`). Leg 1 — membership: a reviewer or merge delegate whose `producer_ref` is a member of the candidate's attested `producer_refs[]`, any member and not only the first listed. Leg 2 — **product inequality**: a reviewer/delegate whose `producer_ref` is NOT a member, but whose `identity_class.product` equals the `product` of some `p` in `producer_refs[]` while differing in `account` and/or `process_class` — the whole-`identity_class`-inequality reading would admit it; the implemented predicate DENYs it, and the control asserts the DENY.
13. **Empty-producer-set refusal:** a `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` envelope whose `producer_refs[]` is empty satisfies no independence requirement; the gate DENYs exactly as if the evidence were absent (§4.2 complete-set contract; TD v0.4 §8.3 count rule).
14. **Observed-authorship scoping:** in a deployment whose policy declares the §4.2 git-identity alias binding contract, a reviewer absent from the attested `producer_refs[]` but matching, through the declared alias binding, an author/committer identity observed in the PEP's `GIT_PUSH` bundle walk is refused — the alias-extended member enters the basis and is compared by the same predicate, i.e. by membership AND by `identity_class.product` inequality against the reviewer's `product`, not by whole-`identity_class` inequality; in a deployment WITHOUT the alias contract, the control verifies the converse — that **no K2 evidence and no gate input exists from authorship at all**: observed author/committer strings appear only as product diagnostics, no `EvidenceEnvelopeV1` of any kind is sealed from them, and no admission decision changes when they are present, absent, or altered (§4.2 observed authorship, diagnostics-only).
15. **Self-report cannot open the machine-merge path:** an external candidate whose only provenance is `SELF_REPORT` + `AUTHENTICATED_SOURCE` `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` — even one whose `producer_refs[]` satisfies the §4.2 product predicate against the delegate — DENYs the delegated `AGENT_DECISION` merge and routes to the `HUMAN_DECISION` path; the control asserts the DENY *and* that the same effect admits on a `HUMAN_DECISION` with correct §9.3 scope, proving the fall-back is a route and not a dead end (§4.2 assurance decision (a)–(c)).
16. **Self-omitting attester refused at ingress:** a `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` submission from a producer registered `source_relation` `SELF_REPORT` whose stamped `producer_ref` is not a member of its own `claim.producer_refs[]` is REFUSED at ingress — no envelope sealed, nothing in K2 (§4.2 assurance decision (d)). The control runs that leg **twice, once at each `provenance.integrity` level** — `AUTHENTICATED_SOURCE` and a signature-verified `SIGNED_ATTESTATION` — and asserts the identical refusal, pinning the rule's integrity-blindness so a signed attester cannot omit itself; and it asserts the converse scope, that a producer registered `INDEPENDENT_OBSERVATION` submitting `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` in which it is (correctly) absent is ADMITTED. The control also asserts the stated insufficiency: a *third-party* omission (attester P1 names `[P1]` while P2 also produced content) is NOT refused by this rule, so the control records it as an open shape closed only by (a)–(c).
17. **Conflicting provenance DENYs the gate:** two sealed `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` envelopes bound to the same `{ repo_id, candidate_sha }` with non-identical claim digests cause every independence-requiring gate for that candidate to DENY — no picking the policy-designated producer's envelope, no union, no latest-wins (§4.2 consumption rule (c)). Companion leg: two envelopes with *identical* claim digests do not DENY.
18. **Omission from assembly is itself non-conformant:** the control constructs an admission input that omits a sealed `CANDIDATE_PROVENANCE` envelope bound to the subject — the cherry-picked-`evidence_refs[]` shape — and shows the deployment either refuses the assembly or DENYs the independence gate. A deployment whose assembly silently accepts the subset admits the effect and thereby fails this control (§4.2 consumption rule (b)).
